The current reward logic (reward = current_portfolio_value - prev_portfolio_value) is essentially a
  Mark-to-Market (MtM) PnL approach. 

  Analysis of the Current Logic
   * The "Short-Termist" Trap: Because the agent is rewarded based on the realized (cash) and unrealized (holdings)
     change in value from step t to $t+1$, it becomes "myopic." If the agent sells at t, it realizes the current
     gain. If it holds, it incurs the risk of price fluctuation until $t+1$. In the agent's eyes, the "safest" way
     to secure a positive reward is to sell while the asset is above its purchase price.
   * Why it works for some models: It is mathematically simple and ensures that if the portfolio grows, the agent
     is rewarded. It effectively trains the agent to seek out assets that appreciate.
   * The Problem: Your observation is correct: selling at t to lock in a small gain when the asset continues to
     rise at $t+1$ means the agent "missed out." However, the agent doesn't "know" the future price. The current
     reward function treats a "Sell and hold cash" decision identically to a "Hold asset" decision if they result
     in the same MtM value.

  Proposed Improvement: Risk-Adjusted / Alpha-Seeking Reward
  To discourage "premature" selling, you can modify the reward to incorporate an Opportunity Cost or a Holding
  Premium.

  1. The "Excess Return" Approach
  Instead of rewarding the raw PnL, reward the agent for beating a passive baseline (like holding the asset).

   1 # Instead of: reward = portfolio_value - prev_portfolio_value
   2 # Use:
   3 market_return = (next_price - current_price) / current_price
   4 # Reward = (Portfolio_Change_Pct - Market_Return_Pct)
  This forces the agent to demonstrate that its decision (to hold, buy, or sell) outperformed the asset's natural
  movement.

  2. The "Holding Incentive" (Decay)
  If you want to reduce the frequency of churn, you can add a small "holding cost" or "liquidity penalty":

   1 # Penalty for being in cash vs invested
   2 if action_type == 0 and len(self.positions) > 0:
   3     reward += holding_bonus  # Small incentive to keep the asset if it's trending

  3. The "PnL Smoothing" (Holding)
  Reward the agent for the total return over the entire duration of the hold, rather than individual step-by-step
  PnL. This is much harder to train (as it requires delayed rewards) but often yields "smarter" agents.

  Recommendation
  If your goal is to prevent premature selling:
   1. Introduce a "Holding Alpha" reward: If the agent sells and the asset price subsequently increases, the agent
      receives a negative "opportunity cost" reward.
   2. Add a Trade Fee/Commission: If not already present, adding a small transaction cost (reward -= fee) is the
      most effective way to kill "quick-win" churning. It forces the agent to only sell when it believes the profit
      significantly outweighs the cost of the trade.
